Colour

The colour table maps the scaled values to colours. Which one you choose is mostly taste, with one real constraint: a table that is not monotonic in lightness will invent structure that is not in your data.

The tables

  • Gray, Red, Green, Blue, A, B, BB, HE, I8 — the classic set.
  • AIPS0, SLS, HSV, Heat, Cool, Rainbow, Standard, Staircase, Color — the rest of the traditional tables, several inherited from AIPS.
  • Viridis, Plasma, Inferno and Magma at the top level, with the rest of matplotlib's grouped below as Matplotlib Uniform, Sequential, Diverging and Cyclic.
  • h5utils, Cubehelix, Gist, Topographic, Scientific Colour Maps and Solar Colormaps — further collections, 164 bundled tables in all.
  • User — Load Colormap reads .sao and .lut files, so tables you already have work here.
For publication, prefer grey or one of the perceptually uniform maps. Rainbow and HSV have bright bands in the middle that read as edges in the data, and they do not survive being printed in black and white or being seen by a colour-blind reader.

Inverting, and resetting

Color → Invert Colormap flips the table — black-on-white for a figure that will be printed. Reset Colormap returns the table to its default, and Reset Contrast/Bias undoes a right-drag without changing which table you are on.

The colour bar

Color → Colorbar and View → Colorbar show and hide it; they are the same switch in two places, which is a deliberate divergence from DS9, where it lives only in View. Color → Colorbar Options sets its orientation, whether it carries numbers, the font, the size of the bar and the number of ticks. View → Multiple Colorbars gives each frame its own when they are tiled.

Colour tags

Color → Color Tags paints a chosen range of values in a flat colour of its own, on top of the table. Use it to mark saturated pixels, or to pick out everything above a threshold without changing the stretch.

Colormap Parameters

Contrast and bias as numbers rather than as a drag, with a preview of the table. Useful when you need two frames to have demonstrably the same settings, or when you want to write the numbers into a caption.
