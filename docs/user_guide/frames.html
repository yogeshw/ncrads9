Frames and Comparison

A frame holds one image with its own scale, colour table, pan, zoom and regions. Several frames let you compare — which is most of what a viewer is for.

Making and moving between frames

  • Frame → New Frame (Ctrl+N), and the RGB, HSV, HLS and 3D variants.
  • Delete Frame (Ctrl+W), Delete All Frames.
  • Tab and Shift+Tab step through them; First, Previous, Next, Last and Goto Frame do the same from the menu.
  • Show/Hide Frames takes a frame out of the tiling without deleting it; Move Frame reorders them.
  • Clear Frame empties one, Reset Frame returns its settings to the defaults, Refresh Frame redraws it.

Four ways to show several frames

ModeWhat it doesGood for
Single FrameOne at a time.Detail.
Tile FramesAll at once, side by side. Comparing morphology, or several bands of the same field.
Blink FramesCycles through them in place. Finding what moved or changed — the oldest trick in observational astronomy, and still the best one for a transient.
Fade FramesCross-fades between them. The same, more gently, when the two differ in alignment.

Frame Parameters sets the tile layout — grid, columns, rows — and the blink interval.

Opening several files at once — ncrads9 *.fits, or picking more than one in the Open dialog — loads each into its own frame and tiles them, so you see them all at the start. Click a tile to make that frame the current one; it takes a green outline, and from then on the usual controls — zoom, colormap, scale, and so on — act on it, its tile updating in place. Zoom while tiled magnifies the selected frame within its tile; Zoom Fit returns it to filling the tile.

Match and Lock

These are the two halves of keeping frames comparable, and the difference matters:

  • Frame → Match makes the other frames match this one now, once.
  • Frame → Lock keeps them matched from now on, so a change to one is a change to all.

Both work on frame, crosshair, crop, slice, bin, axes order, scale, scale and limits, colorbar, block, smooth and 3D, and both can tie the view together by Image, Physical, Amplifier, Detector or WCS coordinates. Locking by WCS is what you want for two images of the same field from different instruments: pan one and the other follows to the same piece of sky, whatever its pixel grid.

Bin and Smooth locking is a known divergence: those two settings are the window's here rather than each frame's, so all frames already share them and the entry says so rather than silently doing nothing.

Colour composites

An RGB frame holds three images, one per channel. Frame → RGB opens the dialog that chooses the current channel, shows or hides each, and locks the channels' scale and colour together. HSV and HLS work the same way in those colour spaces. Channels need not share a pixel grid if they have WCS — lock by WCS and they will be aligned.

Data cubes

A cube arrives with the Cube dialog (Frame → Cube): a slider to step through planes, playback controls to run through them, and the axis order for cubes whose spectral axis is not the third. Analysis tools act on the plane you are looking at.

3D frames

Frame → New Frame 3D renders a cube as a volume rather than a stack, with azimuth and elevation, a choice of method, and a background setting. Frame → 3D opens its controls.
